# Phase 1: Candidate Experience

What a candidate sees, does and is asked in the Phase 1 prototype. Phase 1 proves the core of the product: the app finds the experiences in a candidate's background that matter most for the role, then coaches the candidate to turn each one into a story they can tell and defend in an interview. The CV is only raw material: the output is interview stories, never a rewritten CV.

**Status: revision 2, approved by the founder on 14 October 2026 (D-29 to D-36).** Revision 1 was approved on 13 October 2026 (D-23). The founder's walkthrough on 14 October found it mechanical, unclear about what to do next and judged from the interviewer's side rather than the candidate's. This revision keeps the six steps and the go or no-go test, and changes how each step speaks to the candidate. The decisions are listed at the end.

## Scope and journey

**In Phase 1:** stories US-02, 16, 17, 18, 44, 45 and 46, plus the redaction summary (US-04) built in Phase 0.

**Not yet:** organisation research, question prediction, mock interviews and payments. The job ad is read only lightly in Phase 1, enough to know what the role would expect; full role decoding arrives in Phase 2.

**Worked example used throughout:** a candidate with eight years in logistics operations applying for a Supply Chain Manager role.

A step tracker sits at the top of every screen, so the candidate always knows where they are and what comes next:

**1. Your role and CV · 2. Your strongest material · 3. Build your stories · 4. Check for gaps · 5. Prepare for challenges · 6. Where you stand**

| Step | Candidate's question | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | "What is this, and what do I need?" | What the app does, what to have ready, how long it takes |
| 1. Your role and CV | "Where do I begin?" | Paste the job ad, upload the CV, see what personal details were removed |
| 2. Your strongest material | "What do I have that this role needs?" | The 8 to 10 experiences that matter most for this role, with how ready each is to tell |
| 3. Build your stories | "How do I turn these into stories I can tell?" | One story at a time, a few questions written for that story, the story taking shape as they answer |
| 4. Check for gaps | "Am I missing anything?" | What the role usually asks for, a verdict, and one question for each gap |
| 5. Prepare for challenges | "What will an interviewer push back on?" | Claims likely to be challenged, each with a question written for that claim |
| 6. Where you stand | "Am I ready?" | Stories ready to tell, what is left, and what comes next |

The whole journey should take about 30 minutes. The candidate can stop at any point and pick up where they left off.

## Welcome

Shown once, before anything is pasted or uploaded.

> **Get ready for your interview, using what you've already done.**
>
> Interviewers ask for real examples: what you did, what changed because of it, and how you know. Most CVs list the work but not the stories behind it. In about 30 minutes we'll:
>
> 1. read the job ad to see what this role needs,
> 2. find the experiences in your CV that best show it,
> 3. help you turn the most important ones into stories you can tell with confidence,
> 4. check for gaps and for claims an interviewer is likely to question.
>
> **Have ready:** the job ad (copied from the listing) and your CV (PDF or Word).
>
> You can stop at any time and come back. [How we handle your information]

Then the privacy consent, then **Start**.

## Step 1: Your role and CV

**The candidate sees** two clearly numbered parts on one page:

> **1. Paste the job ad.** Copy the full listing, including the duties and requirements. We use it to work out what this role needs.
>
> **2. Upload your CV.** PDF or Word. We use it to find the experiences that show what you bring.

Part 2 opens once part 1 is saved. Under the CV box: *"We remove personal details an employer shouldn't use, and we never keep your original file."*

**After upload** the app shows what it removed (approved wording, unchanged):

> *We removed 3 things you don't need for interview preparation: your photo, your date of birth and a line about your health. We don't use them, and we delete them within 24 hours (D-27). Restore anything we removed by mistake.*

Then one clear button: **Next: find your strongest material**.

**Rules:** warn if the job ad looks cut off; if the CV cannot be read, say so plainly and suggest a different file type.

## Step 2: Your strongest material

**The candidate sees** the 8 to 10 experiences that matter most for this role, best first, under a headline:

> *Here are the 9 experiences from your CV that best show what a Supply Chain Manager role needs. 2 are ready to tell, 4 are almost there and 3 are worth building. In the next step we'll strengthen them, one at a time.*

Each card shows the experience in the candidate's words, a rating label, what is already strong, and the single most useful thing to add:

| Label | Meaning | Example card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ready to tell** | Result, your part and how you know are all there; it will hold up to follow-up questions | "Cut warehouse picking errors from 4% to 0.8% in 9 months by redesigning slotting." *Strong: a clear before and after, and your action. Have the measure ready if asked.* |
| **Almost there** | A real result, missing one or two details an interviewer will ask for | "Reduced freight costs significantly by renegotiating carrier contracts." *Strong: a clear result and your action. Next: roughly how much, so "significantly" becomes a figure.* |
| **Worth building** | Describes the job more than what changed because of you | "Responsible for a team of 12 in the dispatch centre." *Next: what changed while you led the team? That is your story.* |

The labels and the "Strong" and "Next" lines speak to the candidate, not about them. Each card names one next step only, chosen for that experience: a timeframe or a starting point is not asked for where it would not matter.

Below the main cards, a collapsed section: *"22 more experiences from your CV"*, which the candidate can open and add to their stories.

One clear button: **Next: build your stories**.

**Rules:** ratings are never inflated to encourage, and never deflated either: a figure like "reduced external IT spend by NZ$2M a year" is recognised as a strong result even before it is built out. Scoring accuracy is proven by the calibration set before testers see it.

## Step 3: Build your stories

**The candidate works through** the chosen experiences one at a time, with a progress line: *"Story 3 of 9: Reduced freight costs by renegotiating carrier contracts."*

The screen has two halves:

- **Your story so far:** the story in Situation, Action and Result form (the STAR shape interviewers use), built only from the candidate's own words, filling in as they answer. Gaps show as a short prompt, for example *"Result: how much did costs fall?"*
- **The next question:** one question at a time, written for this story and starting from what the CV already says.

**How questions are chosen:** the app reads what the experience already says and asks only what is genuinely missing and matters for this story, in this order of importance: what changed, your part, how you know, then the detail an interviewer would probe. At most four questions per story, usually two or three. Each question names what it is building on, so the candidate knows why it is asked:

> *You said the business got a single view of spend against value. What did decisions look like before that, and what changed once leaders had it?*

> *You led the carrier renegotiation. Roughly how much did freight costs fall, and over what period? An estimate is fine; we'll label it as one.*

**Answering:** the candidate types an answer, or chooses **Skip this one**. The app then shows the updated story with one line: *"Did we get this right?"* with **Yes, looks right** (saves and moves on) and **Edit** (to change any part in their own words). Nothing is saved that the candidate has not seen.

**Estimates:** when an answer has a figure, the app asks once: *"Is that exact, or your estimate?"* An estimate asks *"How did you arrive at it?"* and is labelled in the story: "(estimate, based on monthly KPI reports)".

**Worked example:** "Responsible for a team of 12 in the dispatch centre." The app asks: *"While you led the dispatch team, what improved because of how you ran it?"* The candidate answers that on-time dispatch improved, from about 85% to about 96% over 18 months, an estimate from monthly KPI reports. The story now reads: *Situation: a 12-person dispatch team. Action: [what you changed]. Result: on-time dispatch rose from about 85% to about 96% over 18 months (estimate, based on monthly KPI reports).* The app asks one more question for the missing action, and the label moves from Worth building to Almost there or Ready to tell.

**For graduates (PE-1)** questions are framed around study, volunteer and part-time work: *"In your club treasurer role, what changed because you were there?"*

When the last story is done, or the candidate chooses **Finish for now**: **Next: check for gaps**.

**Rules:** the label updates only when the candidate adds something new; skipped is recorded as "not answered", never as "no"; the app never invents or rounds a number.

## Step 4: Check for gaps

**The candidate sees** a verdict first, then the detail:

> *This role usually asks for 8 things. Your stories already show 5. There are 3 we didn't find. If you've done them, tell us and we'll add them to your stories.*

| What the role asks for | Where it comes from | In your stories? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier and carrier management | In the ad | Yes: carrier contract renegotiation |
| Inventory planning and forecasting | In the ad | Not found |
| Budget responsibility | Typical for this role | Not found |
| Leading teams through change | In the ad | Yes: dispatch team |

**For each one not found** the app asks one question, with clear answers:

> *Supply Chain Manager roles usually involve inventory planning or forecasting. Have you done this?*
>
> **Yes, I have** · **No, I haven't** · **Not sure**

- **Yes, I have:** the app asks for a short example and adds it to Build your stories.
- **No, I haven't:** recorded as a gap. Preparing an honest answer for a gap comes in a later phase.
- **Not sure:** kept as an open question.

**Rules:** expectations come from the job ad first, then from what similar roles usually require, each shown with where it came from. A missing item is never treated as a gap until the candidate answers.

## Step 5: Prepare for challenges

**The candidate sees** a short list of claims an interviewer is likely to push back on. Each card separates the claim from the question:

> **From your CV:** "Cut transport costs by 70% in one year."
>
> **An interviewer might ask:** *That's a large saving. How was it measured, and what made it possible?*

| Type | When it is raised | Example question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Needs a figure | A claimed improvement with no size, where a size would matter | *"Significantly improved warehouse efficiency": what did you measure it by, and roughly how much did it improve?* |
| Dates | Two roles overlap by rule | *Your roles at Acme and Bolt overlap from March to August 2021. Did you hold both at once, or is one date out?* |
| Unusually large | A figure well above what is typical | *That's a large saving. How was it measured, and what made it possible?* |
| Your part | "We" with no clear personal role | *What was your own part in rolling out the new WMS?* |

Each question is written for that claim and is only raised where it fits: a claim about restructuring roles is not asked "by how much". The candidate can answer, correct the claim in their own words, or mark it **This is fine as it is**.

**Rules:** the tone is that of a helpful colleague preparing them, never an accusation. An unusually large figure is never changed or removed by the app.

## Step 6: Where you stand

**The candidate sees** what they have, in plain words:

> *You now have 6 stories ready to tell and 2 almost there, up from 2 ready when you started. Your strongest for this role: carrier renegotiation, picking errors and the dispatch team turnaround. Still open: inventory planning (you said you haven't done it) and one claim to check.*

Below it, the candidate's stories in full, ready to read before the interview, with a **Copy my stories** button.

Then an honest next step:

> *That's as far as this test version goes. Next, we'll match your stories to the questions this role is likely to ask, and let you practise them.*

And the two-question feedback request used for the go or no-go decision: *"How useful were the questions we asked?"* (1 to 5) and *"What would you change?"*

**Rules:** counts compare like with like: "when you started" and "now" cover the same stories, and stories the candidate added are reported separately.

## Rules the app always follows

- Never invents or rounds up a number; estimates are always labelled, with how the candidate arrived at them.
- Never raises a rating without something new from the candidate.
- Never treats a skipped question or a missing item as a "no".
- Asks one question at a time, in plain words, written for that story, and only what is still needed.
- Honest, not flattering: a story that would not hold up is called "worth building", with the one thing that would fix it.
- Never changes the candidate's words without showing them first.
- Always shows where the candidate is and what the next step is.

## Go or no-go test

Unchanged. Phase 1 is tested by invited team members (D-26, D-28). Phase 2 starts only if all four thresholds are met:

| Measure | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Usefulness of the questions | At least 70% of testers rate them 4 or 5 out of 5 |
| Evidence improvement | At least half of the stories worked on move up one label or more |
| Completion | At least 80% of testers finish within 45 minutes |
| Scoring accuracy | The AI-only calibration set passes (D-16) |

## Decisions in this revision

| ID | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| D-29 | Add a welcome page (what the app does, what to have ready, how long it takes) and a step tracker on every screen with plain step names. |
| D-30 | Rating labels speak to the candidate: Ready to tell (6 to 7), Almost there (4 to 5), Worth building (1 to 3), replacing Interview-ready, Needs detail and Not yet a story. Each card shows what is strong and one next step, not a list of everything missing. |
| D-31 | Step 2 leads with the 8 to 10 experiences that matter most for the role; the rest are available but collapsed. |
| D-32 | Story questions are written by the model for each story, starting from what the CV already says, asking only what matters for that story (at most four). This replaces the fixed six-question order and template-only wording. The no-flattery and number checks stay enforced in code on every question shown. |
| D-33 | The story builds visibly in STAR form as the candidate answers; confirmation reads "Did we get this right?" with a one-click Yes. |
| D-34 | Step 4 opens with a verdict, and its answers are "Yes, I have", "No, I haven't" and "Not sure". |
| D-35 | Claim-check questions are written for each claim and raised only where they fit; the claim and the question are shown separately, and the candidate can mark a claim as fine. |
| D-36 | The summary shows stories ready to tell, compares like with like, lets the candidate copy their stories, and states plainly where the test version ends. |
